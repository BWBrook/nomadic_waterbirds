# Manuscript Plan

## Target Contribution

The paper should present Australia's nomadic waterbirds as a test case for how
mobile communities track pulsed, climate-sensitive wetland resources. The
central contribution is not a new atlas or a static SDM. It is a
multi-species, observation-aware estimate of hydrological reaction norms and
wetland ecological roles through time.

## Likely Framing

The best version is an ecological synthesis for a high-quality ecology or
conservation journal. A methods framing is appropriate only if the
observation-model, validation, or bias-integration contribution becomes the
dominant novelty.

Possible journal neighbourhoods include applied ecology, conservation ecology,
global change ecology, biogeography, ecological applications, and methods
journals if the workflow itself becomes the main contribution.

## Central Claims And Required Evidence

| Claim | Evidence required before manuscript use |
| --- | --- |
| Species and guilds differ in hydrological lag structure. | Species and guild reaction curves with uncertainty, model comparison or posterior evidence for lag terms, and blocked validation performance. |
| Wetting novelty after dry periods matters for nomadic species. | Novelty metric definition, interpretable effect estimates, sensitivity to absolute wet area, and hydrological-event validation. |
| Wetland value is context-dependent. | Local wetness by regional water-availability response summaries and validation across scarcity versus widespread-water periods. |
| Drought refugia and breeding engines are different ecological roles. | Separate role probabilities, independent breeding or nesting evidence where possible, and uncertainty-aware maps. |
| Citizen-science data can support reaction-norm inference when observation-aware. | Effort/detectability model, sampling-bias stress test, and independent validation against standardised or aerial-survey data where feasible. |

No claim should be upgraded from hypothesis to manuscript result until the
listed evidence exists.

## Figure Plan

1. Conceptual model: pulsed water, lagged bird response, regional scarcity,
   drought-refuge role, and breeding-support role.
2. Hydrological reaction norms for representative species and guilds, including
   current water, lagged water, novelty, persistence, or drying as appropriate.
3. Wetland ecological-role map with uncertainty, showing drought refugia,
   flood-pulse foraging wetlands, breeding-support wetlands, stepping-stones,
   variable-use wetlands, and low-response/uncertain wetlands.
4. Hindcast redistribution across contrasting hydrological periods, such as
   severe drought, post-drought flooding, broad flood years, and drying phases.

## Table Plan

- Species and guild table: taxonomy, guild, breeding strategy, conservation
  context, and inclusion rationale.
- Validation table: block type, held-out scope, endpoint, metric, and result.
- Wetland-role summary table: high-confidence sites, uncertainty, and evidence
  basis.
