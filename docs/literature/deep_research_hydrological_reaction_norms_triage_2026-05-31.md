## High-Value Takeaways

- The "hydrological reaction norm" framing is defensible if presented as an
  ecological response-curve analogy, not a narrow quantitative-genetic claim.
- The paper must distinguish occurrence, relative abundance, breeding,
  recruitment, drought-refuge use, and post-breeding movement. Treating all of
  these as generic "waterbird response" would weaken the manuscript.
- The hydrology axes in the current workflow are well supported by the review:
  current wetness, antecedent wetness, hydroperiod persistence, drying
  velocity, re-wetting novelty, and regional water availability.
- The current parent/support/subunit ladder is scientifically necessary.
  Ramsar boundaries, DEA Waterbodies, and named wetland features are different
  objects and should not be collapsed into one convenient unit layer.
- Citizen-science streams should be modelled through an observation process,
  with complete checklists, effort covariates, site-selection bias, and
  conspicuous-species bias kept visible.
- Eastern Australian Waterbird Survey and related aerial-survey products are
  unusually valuable as independent validation or calibration evidence, but
  should not be treated as simple truth tables.
- Validation should include hydrological-event blocking, not only spatial or
  temporal blocking.
- The publishable novelty is dynamic, observation-aware, multi-response
  hydrological reaction norms. A generic static SDM with water area as one
  predictor would likely be incremental.

## Main Risks To Guard Against

- Overstating novelty from a broad review. The report says it did not find an
  obvious exact published analogue, but that is not a systematic proof of
  absence.
- Using secondary URLs, PDFs, ResearchGate entries, or web summaries as final
  citation evidence.
- Allowing the phrase "false refugia" to sound like an established Australian
  formal category unless directed verification supports that use.
- Treating DEA Waterbodies as complete wetland habitat, especially for
  vegetated floodplain and pulse-driven systems.
- Treating breeding records as simple presences rather than threshold,
  persistence, and nesting-condition responses.
